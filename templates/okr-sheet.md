# OKR Sheet

> Template. Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.

A one-quarter OKR sheet for a team of one. The playbook notes there is no rigorous evidence that OKRs improve outcomes; treat them as a menu of rituals. What transfers: a few written priorities, numeric key results with baselines, and an honest score at quarter end. Use it at the quarterly reset and glance at it in the weekly review.

## Rules (from the playbook)

- 1-3 objectives per quarter, 2-4 key results (KRs) each. More than about nine KRs in total is a to-do list.
- An objective is a qualitative outcome a customer or the business would notice, not a project ("Build onboarding" is a project).
- A KR is a measurable outcome with a baseline and target. No baseline? Make "establish the baseline" the first KR.
- Label each KR committed (expect 1.0) or stretch (expect about 0.7; Google's 0.6-0.7 guidance applies to stretch goals).
- Write your scoring rule before the quarter starts, so you do not flatter yourself afterwards.
- Tasks live in a separate list; OKRs are not a task list.

## Illustrative examples (made-up numbers; not benchmarks)

Pre-revenue:

| Objective | Key results |
|---|---|
| O1: Confirm that a specific buyer will pay to solve problem X | KR1: 20 problem interviews completed (from 0). KR2: 3 written pre-orders or paid pilots at the target price. KR3: top-3 stated pain points documented and ranked |
| O2: Ship a usable first version | KR1: 10 users complete the core task unaided. KR2: median time-to-first-value under 10 minutes (baseline measured in week 2) |

Revenue stage:

| Objective | Key results |
|---|---|
| O1: Make the product stick | KR1: month-2 logo retention from 70% to 80%. KR2: 60% of new users reach the activation event within 7 days (from 45%). KR3: halve cases of the top churn reason (identify it in week 1) |
| O2: Grow efficiently | KR1: MRR from 4k to 6k. KR2: CAC payback under 12 months on the best channel. KR3: runway never below 9 months |

## Common failure modes

- Objectives written as projects; KRs that measure activity (posts published) rather than outcomes (signups from posts).
- No baseline, so scores are guesses.
- Set and forget: never reviewed between planning sessions.
- Vanity metrics: if you cannot name a decision a metric would change, drop it.
