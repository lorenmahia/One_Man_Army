# Investor Landscape and Approach: Who Funds Early-Stage Startups and How to Reach Them

> Last verified: 2026-11-11. Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.

## Summary

- **Fact:** Venture capital is a niche funding route, not the default. A company is a "startup" in the venture sense only if it is designed to grow fast, and funding is not required for that ([Startup = Growth, Paul Graham, Sept 2012 - older than two years](https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)). Revenue-first and bootstrapping are valid outcomes.
- **Fact:** The market is bifurcated in 2026. US venture deal value reached USD 515.8B through Q3 2026, with AI taking 82.7% and $100M+ deals about 75% of Q3 value ([NVCA/PitchBook Venture Monitor Q3 2026](https://nvca.org/pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor/); [SaaStr on PitchBook data](https://www.saastr.com/a-record-8625-startups-raised-their-first-venture-round-this-year-smaller-vcs-now-have-to-find-founders-before-they-found-anything/)). First-time funds are scarce (81 closed through Q3 2026).
- **Fact:** Standard accelerator deals are published: YC is USD 500K (USD 125K for 7% plus USD 375K uncapped MFN SAFE) ([YC Deal](https://ycombinator.com/deal)); Techstars is USD 220K (USD 200K MFN SAFE plus USD 20K for 5%) with regional variants ([Techstars terms](https://www.techstars.com/investment-terms)).
- **Estimate:** Solo founders are about a third of new US startups but about half that share of those that close a VC round (35% vs 17%, 2024 cohort) ([Carta via Funds Society, Mar 2025](https://www.fundssociety.com/en/news/alternatives/las-startups-dirigidas-por-un-fundador-individual-se-han-mas-que-duplicado-pero-tienen-menos-exito-a-la-hora-de-conseguir-capital-riesgo/)). Plan to over-prove traction rather than rely on pedigree.
- **Fact:** Outside the US, the legal wrapper often decides eligibility: YC invests only in US, Canada, Cayman or Singapore corporations, and Techstars requires US or approved-country entities, so a "flip" and its tax cost must be priced before you apply.

## 1. Should you raise at all? Fit test

**Fact.** Venture funding is a minority route. Older Kauffman-cited research found under 5% of startup funding came from VCs and 6.5% of fast-growing Inc. 5000 firms had raised VC ([Kauffman, May 2016 - older than two years](https://www.kauffman.org/currents/three-facts-you-probably-didnt-know-about-and-venture-capital-and-entrepreneurship/amp/)). A current figure is not verified; see Open questions. PitchBook estimates 8,625 US startups closed a first-ever venture round through Q3 2026, a record ([SaaStr citing PitchBook, Q3 2026](https://www.saastr.com/a-record-8625-startups-raised-their-first-venture-round-this-year-smaller-vcs-now-have-to-find-founders-before-they-found-anything/)).

**Fact.** Venture returns are concentrated: secondary summaries of Correlation Ventures data report about 65% of financings returned less than the capital invested ([VC Beast power-law explainer, secondary](https://vcbeast.com/venture-capital-power-law-explained)). **Inference:** funds therefore need each investment to have a credible path to a very large outcome, which is why they pass on good small businesses.

**Fit test (answer honestly; 5+ "venture" answers suggests VC may fit).**

| Question | Points toward venture | Points toward bootstrapping / revenue-first |
|---|---|---|
| Market size | Large market where a winner takes much of it | Niche or local market with solid margins |
| Growth shape | Needs capital before revenue (R&D, network effects, hardware, regulation) | Customers pay early; growth funded by cash flow |
| Growth speed | Can plausibly grow several percent per week for a sustained period ([PG: 5-7% weekly is good at YC, 2012](https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)) | Steady 20-100% per year is the realistic ceiling |
| Unit economics | Gross margin and payback improve with scale | Already profitable per customer today |
| Your goal | Build to a large exit or IPO and accept dilution and board input | Own the company, pay yourself, keep control |
| Evidence you hold | Waitlist, pilots, retention, revenue | Paying customers and repeat orders |

**Inference.** Service businesses, agencies, local retail, most consultancies and lifestyle software can be excellent businesses and poor venture candidates. Also note that "raising" is a distraction cost: YC advises separating building from fundraising and running a concentrated window ([Aaron Harris, YC, Aug 2019 - older than two years](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)).

**Fact.** Profitable startups may still raise to grow faster than revenue allows ([PG, 2012](https://paulgraham.com/growth.html)). Raising is a choice about speed, not a sign of legitimacy.

**So what for a solo founder:** Decide in writing whether you want a venture-sized outcome; if not, skip to non-dilutive and revenue-first options and treat investors as optional.

## 2. Funding sources by stage

Figures are snapshots; confirm on the provider's current page before relying on them.

### 2.1 Context numbers (US)

- **Seed size and valuation.** PitchBook-derived figures reported in 2026: median pre-seed pre-money USD 12M, median seed pre-money USD 18.3M, median seed check USD 3.0M, which buys roughly 14% ([SaaStr on PitchBook, Q3 2026](https://www.saastr.com/a-record-8625-startups-raised-their-first-venture-round-this-year-smaller-vcs-now-have-to-find-founders-before-they-found-anything/)). Carta reported a median primary seed pre-money of USD 15.6M in Q2 2025 ([Carta State of Private Markets Q2 2025 - over one year old](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q2-2025/)). **Range: USD 15.6M (Carta, Q2 2025) to USD 18.3M (PitchBook-derived, 2026);** definitions differ.
- **Pre-seed.** Carta reports an average pre-seed instrument of about USD 276K, up 27% year on year, across 11,500+ SAFEs and notes in Q2 2026, and 91% of SAFEs were post-money ([Carta pre-seed Q2 2026](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2026-full-report/); figure taken from a search summary because the page blocked direct fetch). Treat as unverified at source.
- **Dilution.** YC's older guidance: most seed rounds cost up to about 20%, avoid above 25% ([YC seed guide, Jan 2016 - older than two years](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)). A 2026 PitchBook-derived calculation implies about 14% ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/a-record-8625-startups-raised-their-first-venture-round-this-year-smaller-vcs-now-have-to-find-founders-before-they-found-anything/)). **Range: roughly 14% to 20%** for a typical seed.

### 2.3 Accelerator terms (verify before applying)

- **YC (page undated, (c) 2026).** USD 500K total: USD 125K on a post-money SAFE for a fixed 7%, plus USD 375K on an uncapped MFN SAFE; pro rata right; no milestones; no fees. The page example: at a USD 15M post-money cap the MFN piece converts to 2.5% ([YC Deal](https://ycombinator.com/deal)). **Inference:** total YC ownership around 9.5% at that cap.
- **Techstars (page undated).** USD 220K: USD 200K uncapped MFN SAFE plus USD 20K via a post-money Convertible Equity Agreement converting to 5% common; side letter covers pro rata, drag-along and metrics sharing. Asia-Pacific programs: USD 100K MFN SAFE plus USD 20K CEA, USD 120K total ([Techstars terms](https://www.techstars.com/investment-terms)).
- **Conflict.** Secondary roundups give 6% and USD 120K for some Techstars programs ([Techstars news roundup, Oct 2026; secondary, not opened](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11585)). **Range: 5% (official page) to 6% (secondary); treat the official page as authoritative** and confirm per program.
- **Entity rule.** YC invests only in US, Canada, Cayman or Singapore corporations; Techstars invests in US corporations or foreign equivalents and requires a flip from non-approved countries (both official pages above).

**So what for a solo founder:** Rough rule: angels and micro-VCs for a first cheque, an accelerator only if the entity rules and equity cost fit, non-dilutive money if your technology or revenue qualifies, and avoid debt before you have recurring revenue.

## 3. Researching investors from public sources only

### 3.1 What to extract per investor

1. **Thesis:** sectors, business models, customers, anti-thesis ("we do not invest in...").
2. **Stage and role:** pre-seed, seed, lead or follow; do they take first cheques?
3. **Cheque size:** stated on the firm site, or inferred from the fund size and number of portfolio companies. **Estimate** only.
4. **Geography:** do they back companies outside their home region, and under what entity?
5. **Portfolio conflicts:** direct competitors in the last 3-5 years, adjacent bets, and exits.
6. **Recent activity:** announcements in the last 6-12 months; has the fund recently closed, or is it deploying its last fund? PitchBook notes first-time funds are scarce and 78.1% of 2026 capital went to funds of USD 500M+ ([NVCA Q3 2026](https://nvca.org/pitchbook-nvca-venture-monitor/)).
7. **Founder-friendliness signals:** what portfolio founders say publicly, and the investor's own published guides.

### 3.2 Public source examples

- **Firm websites and portfolio pages:** thesis statements, team, portfolio.
- **SEC Form D on EDGAR:** a notice of an exempt offering, filed within 15 days after the first sale; filing is public and the SEC does not review it for accuracy ([SEC "What is a Form D"](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/what-form-d); [SEC Form D FAQ via search](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-corporation-finance/frequently-asked-questions-answers-form-d)). Late or missing filings are possible because filing is not a condition of the exemption. Funds and startups file Form D; use it to see when a fund closed or a startup's round was reported. EDGAR full-text search indexes Form D XML content, and exemption codes 06b/06c identify Rule 506(b)/(c) ([SEC webmaster FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/files/about/webmaster-faq.htm)). **Inference:** open one sample filing to see which fields it contains before relying on it.
- **Angel networks:** the Angel Capital Association publishes reports and member information ([ACA](https://angelcapitalassociation.org/blog/aca-publishes-2026-angel-funders-report/)).
- **Regulator and aggregate data:** SEC Reg CF statistics; PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor; Carta data pages (these may block automated access).
- **Press and podcasts:** funding announcements, interviews, investor blog posts.
- **Public databases:** commercial and free investor databases exist (for example, OpenVC lists investors with stage and cheque filters; its page blocked direct fetch, so features are unverified). Cross-check any database entry against the firm's own site.
- **Do not:** buy scraped personal contact lists, guess private email addresses, or harvest personal data. Use firm contact forms and public professional channels.

### 3.3 Scoring template for a 30-60 name target list

Score each investor 0-3 per criterion (0 = no/unknown, 3 = strong evidence). Evidence must be public; write the URL in the sheet.

| Criterion | Weight | 0 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thesis fit | x3 | No sector overlap | Explicit thesis names your category |
| Stage fit | x2 | Series A+ only | Invests first cheques at your stage |
| Cheque fit | x2 | Cheque far above/below need | Cheque covers 20-50% of your round |
| Geography/entity fit | x2 | Will not back your jurisdiction | Has backed companies in your country |
| Conflict risk | x2 (invert) | Direct competitor in portfolio | No conflicts |
| Recent activity | x1 | No deals in 12 months | 2+ relevant deals in 6 months |
| Access (warm path) | x2 | No path | Warm intro available |

- **Max score:** (3+2+2+2+2+1+2) x 3 = 42. Tier A: 32+. Tier B: 24-31. Tier C: below 24.
- **Mix for 30-60 names:** about 10-15 Tier A, 15-25 Tier B, rest Tier C or angels who write small cheques. **Inference:** weights are a starting point; adjust after the first five conversations.
- **Process:** contact Tier B first to practise, then Tier A in a compressed window (see section 4).

**So what for a solo founder:** A scored list with URLs turns "pitching everyone" into 30-60 deliberate bets and protects you from polite wasted meetings.

## 4. How to approach

### 4.1 Warm vs cold

- **Fact (older):** YC calls warm introductions the most effective way to meet investors; demo days also help ([YC seed guide, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)).
- **Fact (older):** If you must go cold, keep it short, specific and researched, reference the investor's focus, and make a clear ask ([Aaron Harris, YC, 2019](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)).
- **Inference:** Ask for a forwardable blurb from the introducer, so the investor can decide in under a minute.

### 4.2 What a good first message contains

1. One line on what you do and for whom.
2. One proof point (revenue, pilots, retention, waitlist).
3. Why this investor (a specific thesis or portfolio link).
4. The ask: round size, instrument (for example, a post-money SAFE), and a 20-minute call.
5. A link to a short deck or one-pager, not a large attachment. YC advises offering to pitch first and sending the deck if pressed ([Aaron Harris, 2019](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)).

Keep it under about 120 words (**Inference**).

### 4.3 Follow-up cadence (Inference)

- Day 0: send. Day 5-7: one short follow-up with new progress. Day 14-21: a final note with a milestone. Then pause and add them to a monthly update list.
- YC says persistence works if updates show real momentum ([Aaron Harris, 2019](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)).
- After a meeting: thank-you within 24 hours, answer questions within 48, restate next step.

### 4.4 Running a time-boxed process

- **Fact (older):** Schedule meetings close together because investors talk; do not stack dozens in one day ([Aaron Harris, 2019](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)). Book many meetings and prioritise those likely to close ([YC seed guide, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)).
- **Inference:** Set a 4-6 week window with a start date, a target amount, and a minimum viable round. Pitch angels and micro-VCs first to build momentum, then lead candidates.
- Raise to the next milestone (YC: 12-18 months of progress; Harris: seed runway 18-24 months, avoid over-raising) ([YC, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/); [Harris, 2019](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)).
- Use standard documents; do not negotiate in real time ([YC, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)).

### 4.5 Pipeline tracker (suggested columns)

`Investor | Tier | Score | Source URL | Warm path | First contact | Status | Last touch | Next step + date | Cheque indicated | Notes/conflicts | Decision`

Statuses: Not contacted, Sent, Replied, Meeting booked, Met, Diligence, Term sheet/SAFE, Passed, Closed. Review it every Friday.

### 4.6 Etiquette

- **Fact (older):** YC lists common mistakes: dishonesty, arrogance, slow follow-up, breaking agreements, asking for an NDA, playing investors against each other, negotiating in real time, and taking a "no" personally ([YC seed guide, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)).
- Say no cleanly to investors who are not a fit; give updates to those who said "not yet".
- Never claim commitments you do not have. **Inference:** misstating soft interest as committed money is the fastest way to lose trust.

### 4.7 How investors do diligence on founders

- **Fact (2026-07-30):** Investors test judgment in the first meeting, then take references from former colleagues, reports, co-founders, past investors and sometimes customers, and often go beyond the supplied list through backchannel calls ([CRV, how investors evaluate teams, 2026-07-30](https://www.crv.com/content/how-investors-evaluate-teams)).
- **Fact:** They look for domain fit, speed of execution, coachability, steadiness under pressure, and clear roles ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/how-investors-evaluate-teams)). CRV also says solo founders are increasingly viable if they show output per person and a plan for capability gaps.
- **Do the same to them:** CRV suggests speaking to 5-7 people about a prospective investor, including founders from struggling companies.

### 4.8 Red flags founders should avoid

- Unclear roles, missing technical depth, defensiveness, inconsistent stories; inflated "1% of a huge market" claims ([CRV, 2026](https://www.crv.com/content/how-investors-evaluate-teams)).
- Years on a product with little to show, or no plan for the money ([Harris, 2019](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/aaron-harris-on-fundraising-and-meeting-with-investors)).
- A messy cap table: dead equity from departed co-founders, no vesting ([CRV, 2026](https://www.crv.com/content/how-investors-evaluate-teams)).
- Over-detailed financials too early, or an NDA request ([YC, 2016](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-raise-a-seed-round/)).
- **Investor-side red flag (Harris, 2019):** investors who obscure that they manage outside money. Ask who the investor represents.

**So what for a solo founder:** Treat it like a sales campaign: a scored list, a compact window, weekly pipeline review, and honest answers about gaps.

## 5. Notes for founders outside the US and Europe

**Fact.** Entity rules gate the big programs. YC invests in US, Canada, Cayman or Singapore corporations (others restructure under a parent) ([YC Deal](https://ycombinator.com/deal)); Techstars requires US or equivalent and a flip for non-approved countries such as India ([Techstars terms](https://www.techstars.com/investment-terms)).

**Africa.**
- Partech reports USD 4.1B total African tech funding in 2025 (+25%), USD 2.4B equity across 462 deals and a record USD 1.6B debt (41% of capital); Kenya, South Africa, Egypt and Nigeria took 72% ([Partech Africa 2025 report, 2026-01-22](https://partechpartners.com/news/2025-partech-africa-tech-vc-report-african-tech-funding-rebounds-to-us41b-driven-by-record-debt-activity-and-disciplined-equity-growth)). Seed-plus participation fell 7% (same source). Other trackers report lower totals (see Open questions).
- **Nigeria (single commentary source, Jan 2026; verify):** one analyst argues that Nigeria's 2025 tax acts raised the corporate capital-gains rate from 10% to 30% for medium and large companies and apply indirect-transfer rules that make Delaware flips costly ([Launch Base Africa, 2026-01-26](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/01/26/frictionless-no-more-nigerias-tax-reforms-turn-delaware-flips-into-million-dollar-liabilities/)). This is commentary, not statute; get local tax counsel.

**India.**
- A fast-track merger of a foreign holding company into its Indian subsidiary under Companies Act s.233 / Rule 25A(5) reportedly takes about 3-6 months; a reverse flip is still taxable, and cost grows with valuation, so earlier is cheaper. Secondary reports cite Groww paying about USD 159M of exit tax ([eChai explainer, 2026; secondary](https://echai.ventures/startingup/incorporation-legal/my-company-has-a-delaware-or-singapore-parent-but-all-the-business-is-in-india-should-i-reverse-flip-in-2026-and-what-does-it-cost)). A March 2026 Livemint piece republished by Trilegal reports startups continuing to move headquarters back to India despite delayed IPO timelines ([Trilegal/Livemint, Mar 2026; read via search summary](https://trilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/War-slows-IPOs-but-startups-keep-bringing-headquarters-home.pdf)). Advisory-fee ranges for a reverse flip were not verified (see Open questions).
- **Inference:** the eChai guidance (if most revenue is Indian and there is no US listing plan, consider an Indian company first) is a decision heuristic, not advice.

**Latin America.** LAVCA reports about USD 2.2B in early-stage rounds, 52% of 2025 venture activity (the implied total of about USD 4.2B is our calculation) ([LAVCA 2026 Trends in Tech](https://www.lavca.org/research/2026-lavca-trends-in-tech/)). Crunchbase-based coverage gives about USD 4.1B ([LAVX summary of Crunchbase](https://news.lavx.hu/article/latin-america-vc-funding-rebounds-with-14-3-growth-in-2025-early-stage-investments-surge)).

**Development finance.** IFC's Startup Catalyst invests in accelerators and early-stage funds (not directly in founders), reporting USD 111M invested, 760+ startups and 53 markets as of FY26 ([IFC Startup Catalyst](https://www.ifc.org/en/what-we-do/sector-expertise/venture-capital/startup-catalyst)). Older IFC pages show different totals; use the funds it backs as your target list. **Inference:** DFI-backed funds often have impact or geographic mandates; read them as thesis filters.

**Currency and repatriation.** **Inference:** dollar-denominated investment into a local-currency business creates FX risk for you and for investors, and exchange-control rules decide whether proceeds, dividends and exit gains can leave the country. A secondary article states Nigeria's investment-promotion law guarantees repatriation through authorised dealers ([Fintech Times, secondary](https://thefintechtimes.com/nigerias-fintech-regulatory-landscape-what-global-investors-should-know/)); I have not verified the statute. Check each country with a local lawyer.

**Jurisdiction and flip checklist (Inference):** (1) which investors you target and what entity they require; (2) tax on the flip today versus later; (3) employee equity and IP assignment; (4) exchange-control filings; (5) whether a later reverse flip for a local IPO is plausible.

**So what for a solo founder:** Price your jurisdiction before you apply: the entity you need for YC-style money may carry a tax bill and a regulatory filing that dwarfs the first cheque.
