# Pitch deck and data room for a first-time solo founder

> Last verified: 2026-11-11. Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.

## Summary

- A deck exists to win the next meeting, not the cheque. Keep two versions: a short presented deck and a self-explanatory read-alone deck. **Fact:** DocSend measured investors at roughly two to three minutes per deck in 2022-2023 ([TechCrunch on DocSend, 2022-09](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/22/science-of-pitch-decks/); [TechCrunch on DocSend, 2023-08](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/)).
- Published frameworks agree on the core (problem, solution, traction, business model, team, ask) and differ mainly on length (6 to 11 core slides), whether to lead with traction or "why now", and where market size and competition sit.
- Investor attention has moved toward the team and traction and away from market-size and competition slides ([DocSend report coverage, 2024-12](https://stocktitan.net/news/DBX/v-cs-prioritize-people-in-an-ai-heavy-landscape-according-to-new-z3rqvcox6orq.html)). For a solo founder this makes the team slide the place to address single-founder concerns head-on.
- State the raise as amount, runway and the milestones it buys; size the market bottom-up (customers x price x frequency); show traction as paying, unaffiliated customers and retention rather than cumulative vanity numbers.
- Prepare a data room before the first meeting but open it late and in layers. Cap table mismatches and missing IP assignments are the most repeated causes of diligence delay in the legal sources reviewed.

## 1. What a deck is for

**Fact:** A short pitch rarely wins an investment on the spot; its job is to make investors want a follow-up meeting ([YC guide to Demo Day pitches](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/), undated). DocSend's data frames the same constraint: founders get "just under three minutes" to earn a meeting ([TechCrunch on DocSend, 2022-09-22](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/22/science-of-pitch-decks/)).

Two versions, one story:

| Version | Used for | Text density | Length guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presented deck | Live meeting, demo day, screen-share | Few words per slide; slides support the speaker ([YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)) | Core of about 6-12 slides |
| Read-alone deck | Emailed or link-shared before or after a meeting; forwarded inside a firm | Enough text that it stands without you; short appendix for follow-ups | Same core plus appendix |

**Inference:** The read-alone deck is the one most often judged, because DocSend-style links are forwarded and read without the founder present. Variant's guide makes the same split: decide up front whether the deck is read, presented or both, and put depth in an appendix ([Variant](https://variant.fund/articles/how-to-build-pitch-deck/), undated).

**So what for a solo founder:** Build the story first, then cut it into a presented deck; derive the read-alone deck from it by adding text, not by writing a second story.

### 2.2 Where they differ

- **Length:** 6 core slides (Variant) to 10-11 (Kawasaki, Sequoia, YC, CRV). DocSend measured 16 slides for successful pre-seed decks against 19 for unsuccessful ones in 2023, and about 20 pages as "typical" on an undated Dropbox page ([TechCrunch, 2023-08-30](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/); [Dropbox DocSend research](https://www.dropbox.com/en_GB/resources/docsend-pitch-deck-research)). These figures conflict with the 10-slide rules because DocSend counts appendix and design slides; **Inference.**
- **Traction position:** YC puts it third; Kawasaki has "status and timeline" late; CRV puts it after business model.
- **Market and competition:** core in Sequoia/Kawasaki/CRV, appendix in Variant. DocSend recorded 48% less seed-stage attention on competition slides and 19% less on pre-seed market size in 2024 ([DocSend coverage, 2024-12-18](https://stocktitan.net/news/DBX/v-cs-prioritize-people-in-an-ai-heavy-landscape-according-to-new-z3rqvcox6orq.html)).
- **Terms on the ask:** YC says leave valuation and terms out; CRV puts the amount on the cover.

### 2.3 Recommended order for a solo founder at pre-seed or seed (12 slides)

**Inference:** This is a synthesis, not any firm's template. Keep one idea per slide.

| # | Slide | Question it answers | Evidence investors expect | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title and one-liner | What is this, in one sentence? | Company name, plain-language description, stage | Jargon or a mission statement instead of what the company does ([YC](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/17nFIwCyf2Kz-Ao5HGnmvNZ74L8eSKA2C2Qdaoe-47OM/htmlpresent)) |
| 2 | Problem | Whose pain, how bad, how handled today? | Named customer, a concrete example, interview evidence | Describing the market instead of one person's problem |
| 3 | Solution and product | What do you do, and does it work? | Screenshots or a short demo, concrete benefit | Feature lists; illegible screenshots |
| 4 | Why now | What changed that makes this possible today? | A specific shift (technology, regulation, cost, behaviour) | Trend-name dropping with no link to the product ([Variant](https://variant.fund/articles/how-to-build-pitch-deck/)) |
| 5 | Traction | What proof exists? | Paying unaffiliated customers, MRR trend, retention, or signed LOIs/pilots | Cumulative signups; blurring commitments with conversations ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction)) |
| 6 | Business model and unit economics | How does it make money per customer? | Price, gross margin, CAC and payback assumptions labelled actual or assumed | Margins without costs; mixing one-time fees into recurring revenue |
| 7 | Market (bottom-up) | How big can this get, built from what? | Customers x price x frequency; top-down only as a sanity check | "1% of a huge market" ([Pear VC](https://pear.vc/market-sizing-guide/)) |
| 8 | Competition and alternatives | Why you rather than the status quo or an incumbent? | Honest alternatives table, one durable edge | Claiming no competitors |
| 9 | Go-to-market | How will the next 100 customers be found? | One working channel with measured conversion | "Viral" or "SEO" with no data |
| 10 | Team (solo) | Why you, and how is the single-founder risk covered? | Relevant track record, advisors or first hires, plan for the gaps | Biography list; hiding that you are solo |
| 11 | Ask and milestones | How much, for how long, to reach what? | Amount, runway months, 2-4 measurable milestones, Series A-ready target | Vague "to scale"; no link between amount and milestones |
| 12 | Vision (optional close) | What if it works? | One paragraph, not a forecast | Hockey-stick projections |

Appendix: financial model, cohort tables, product detail, security/privacy summary, detailed competitor grid.

**So what for a solo founder:** Lead with problem-solution-why-now in two or three slides, put traction on slide 5 and the team on slide 10, and move anything that answers a follow-up question to the appendix.

## 3. How investors actually read decks

All figures below are DocSend (Dropbox) data via press or press coverage; DocSend's own report pages returned HTTP 403 when fetched, so exact methodology was not checked. **Flag:** most of these are two to four years old on a fast-moving topic.

| Date | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2020, pre-seed | Funded decks viewed a little over four minutes, unfunded about 1:30 | [Dropbox DocSend research](https://www.dropbox.com/en_GB/resources/docsend-pitch-deck-research) |
| 2021-H1 2022, n=300 pre-seed | Average review 2:42; VC time down 42%; traction time up 41%; product time down 52% | [DocSend press release, 2022-08-02](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-report-on-pre-seed-fundraising-shows-return-to-investor-discipline-imperative-for-startups-to-have-clear-purpose-301597430.html) |
| 2022, n=320 decks | Investors spent 24% less time than 2021; "just under three minutes"; unfunded decks abandoned after 2:13; no unfunded deck had financials | [TechCrunch, 2022-09-22](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/22/science-of-pitch-decks/) |
| 2023 pre-seed | 2:12 vs 2:42 a year earlier; 16 slides (successful) vs 19 (unsuccessful); 65% of unsuccessful decks longer than average | [TechCrunch, 2023-08-30](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/30/pitch-deck-slide-suggestions/) |
| 2024, n>400 pre-seed and seed | 40% more time on seed Team slides, 30% more at pre-seed; seed Competition slide 48% less attention; pre-seed Market Size 19% less; pre-seed rounds averaged 12 weeks | [DocSend coverage, 2024-12-18](https://stocktitan.net/news/DBX/v-cs-prioritize-people-in-an-ai-heavy-landscape-according-to-new-z3rqvcox6orq.html) |

**Conflicting figures:** average deck time is 2:12 (2023 pre-seed, TechCrunch) to 2:42 (2021-22, DocSend); a search summary also reported seed decks dropping under two minutes in 2023, but I could not open that DocSend page, so treat it as unverified. A search summary of DocSend's 2022 seed report put overall VC time at 2:59 (2022) vs 3:16 (2021); unverified (same reason).

**Caveats:** DocSend samples are founders who use DocSend and select into its reporting; funded/unfunded outcomes are self-reported; sample splits are often not published (TechCrunch states none for the 2022 figures). **Inference:** treat these as directional: short, scannable, team and traction visible in under two minutes.

**Design norms (Fact, with sources):** one main point per slide, about seven words or fewer, large fonts, high contrast, no complex or cumulative graphs ([YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)); legibility, simplicity and obviousness are attributed to YC by a third-party guide, not verified against YC.

**So what for a solo founder:** Make slide 1-5 pass a two-minute skim and make traction and team visible without scrolling; cut the deck rather than defend its length.

## 4. Narrative and the ask

**The ask.** CRV's seed guide says founders typically target 18 to 24 months of runway, calculated as cash divided by monthly burn; at a $50,000 monthly burn that is about $0.9M for 18 months or $1.2M for 24 ([CRV seed funding, 2026-03-01](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-funding)). It cites typical seed dilution of 20 to 25%, median 20%. Secondary blogs claim seed-to-A now takes 18 to 36 months and recommend extra buffer; these are weakly sourced ([search summary of Metal, Peony and others, undated](https://www.metal.so/guides/how-much-should-you-raise-a-founders-guide-to-round-sizing-in-2026/)). **Inference:** state the ask as: amount, months of runway, three milestones (for example customers, revenue run-rate, retention) and the Series A evidence they produce. YC advises leaving valuation and cap out of the deck ([YC](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/17nFIwCyf2Kz-Ao5HGnmvNZ74L8eSKA2C2Qdaoe-47OM/htmlpresent)).

**Traction (honest metrics).** CRV (2026-08-03 summary) says seed investors look for paying customers with no personal tie to the founder, ARR that excludes one-time fees, flattening cohort retention, and LTV a multiple of CAC with payback under a year; pre-revenue founders can show signed LOIs, pilots and pre-orders from named companies ([CRV traction](https://www.crv.com/content/startup-traction)). Its seed ARR benchmark of $500K-$1.5M is B2B SaaS-specific; other blogs give $25K-$200K MRR or $5K-$20K+ MRR, so benchmarks range widely ([search summary of CRV, Causo, Forum VC, undated](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/seed-mrr-benchmarks-2026)). **Inference:** label every metric as actual, estimated or assumed, show the monthly trend, and separate commitments from conversations.

**Financials and unit economics.** DocSend found about a quarter of 2022 decks included financials and none of the unsuccessful ones did ([TechCrunch, 2022-09-22](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/22/science-of-pitch-decks/)). Show per-unit revenue and cost, gross margin, payback and a 12-24 month burn plan; keep the full model in the appendix or data room. Use the `unit-economics-model` skill to produce the numbers.

**Team slide for a solo founder.** Carta data (reported via search summaries; Carta pages returned 403): solo-founder share of new startups rose from 23.7% (2019) to 36.3% (H1 2025), but solo-led companies were 30% of 2024 startups and got 14.7% of priced-round cash, and were about half as likely to have VC funding as two-founder teams ([Carta Solo Founders Report 2025, via search summary](https://carta.com/sg/en/data/solo-founders-report/)). Another summary gives 31% (2024) and 36% (2025) ([Carta 2026 Founder Ownership Report, via search summary](https://carta.com/data/founder-ownership-2026-full-report/)). DocSend also saw investors spend more time on Team slides in 2024 and mixed or two-founder teams raise the most ([DocSend coverage, 2024-12-18](https://stocktitan.net/news/DBX/v-cs-prioritize-people-in-an-ai-heavy-landscape-according-to-new-z3rqvcox6orq.html)). **Inference:** address the concern before it is asked: why solo (by choice, not inability to collaborate), who fills the gaps (named advisors, contractors, first hire and when), what evidence shows you can recruit, and a founder-market-fit reason only you could give. Skip advisor logo walls ([YC](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/17nFIwCyf2Kz-Ao5HGnmvNZ74L8eSKA2C2Qdaoe-47OM/htmlpresent)).

**Market sizing.** Pear VC recommends bottom-up (customers x annual revenue per customer) because its assumptions can be tested, and top-down only as a cross-check; it notes tech companies at IPO have typically held 0.1% to 2% of their addressable market, so a 10% share claim undermines credibility ([Pear VC market sizing guide](https://pear.vc/market-sizing-guide/), undated). YC's Demo Day guide likewise asks for price x number of potential customers ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)). **Inference:** show the arithmetic on the slide: who the first reachable segment is, how many, at what price, and what the next segment adds.

**So what for a solo founder:** Make the ask a plan (amount, months, milestones), the market a multiplication you can defend, and the team slide an answer to "what if you get sick, stuck or overloaded?"

## 5. The short live pitch and Q&A

### 5.1 Structure (2-3 minutes)

YC's guide gives an order that is flexible but opens with the introduction and closes with the conclusion: introduction (what you do, lead with traction if strong), problem, product and customer, opportunity (bottom-up), traction, team, business model, conclusion with the 3-4 points to remember. It advises one presenter, a story-first build, rehearsal, and avoiding demos, videos and complex graphs ([YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/)). a16z speedrun founders describe hook, meat and payoff, four one-sentence pillars (problem, solution, founders, traction) and rehearsing with a timer; the source is a 2025 founder-advice post, not a firm guideline ([a16z speedrun, 2025-09-10](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-pitch-in-under-2-min)). The recommended length differs across sources (60 seconds to 2 minutes for Demo Day), so confirm the event's slot; the YC guide gives no duration.

**Inference:** Spoken pitch of about 300 words for two minutes (a vendor guide's figure, unverified); script once, then speak from the five or six points.

### 5.2 The 20 questions founders most often face

**Inference:** This list is a synthesis from the question guides reviewed ([SeedLegals, 2023-06-01](https://seedlegals.com/resources/questions-investors-ask/); [YC Demo Day guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/guide-to-demo-day-pitches/); [CRV seed guide](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-pitch-deck)) and not a ranked frequency study; no source ranked questions by frequency. What a good answer must contain, not a script:

1. What does the company do, and for whom? One sentence, a named customer, no jargon.
2. Why now? A specific change and its date or trend, linked to your product.
3. Why you? Direct personal evidence of founder-market fit.
4. Why are you solo, and what happens if you stall? Choice rationale, gap coverage, hiring plan with trigger points.
5. Who pays today, and how much? Count of paying customers, price, how many are strangers.
6. How did you get your first customers? Channel, conversion rate, hours or spend.
7. What does retention look like? Cohorts by month, churn, and what you changed.
8. What are unit economics today? Price, variable cost, gross margin, payback; actual vs assumed.
9. How big is the market, bottom-up? Customer count, price, frequency, first segment.
10. Who are the competitors and the status quo? Alternatives named honestly, one durable edge.
11. What if a large incumbent or a model provider copies this? Data, workflow, distribution or switching-cost advantage.
12. What is your moat in 3 years? What compounds with usage, not a patent promise.
13. How will you hire? Roles, order, how you source and close.
14. What will you do with the money? Spending by category and months of runway.
15. What milestones does this round reach, and by when? Measurable targets that support a Series A story.
16. What are the biggest risks? Top two or three honestly ranked, with the test for each.
17. What is the valuation or cap you expect, and why? Comparable rounds, flexibility, and instrument preference.
18. What is the long-term vision (five to seven years)? A defensible path, not a number.
19. Are your documents in order (IP assignment, vesting, cap table)? A yes backed by the data room.
20. What is your biggest weakness? A real weakness and what you are doing about it.

**So what for a solo founder:** Rehearse questions 4, 5, 7, 11 and 19 until each answer has a number or a document behind it.

## 6. The data room

**Fact:** Prepare early, share late: early meetings need only the deck and a summary; the full room opens at investment-committee review or term-sheet stage ([CRV data room setup](https://www.crv.com/content/data-room-setup), undated). CRV's seed guide describes seed diligence as basic: incorporation certificate, cap table, prior financings and IP assignments, and calls extensive seed diligence a red flag ([CRV seed funding, 2026-03-01](https://www.crv.com/content/seed-funding)).

### 6.1 Folder structure

CRV suggests numbered folders: 00 Executive Summary, 01 Financials, 02 Legal, 03 Go-to-Market, 04 Product, 05 HR ([CRV data room setup](https://www.crv.com/content/data-room-setup)). A law-firm guide adds regulatory/litigation and prior-financing categories ([Klehr Harrison, undated](https://klehr.com/publications/startup-evolution-a-year-long-journey-through-a-founders-toolkit-due-diligence-ready-building-a-data-room-that-wins-investor-confidence/)). **Inference:** use CRV's six plus `06 Prior financings` and `07 Regulatory and privacy`; date-stamp versions and never leave drafts beside executed copies.

### 6.2 Contents by stage

| Area | Pre-seed (light) | Seed | Series A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate | Certificate of incorporation, bylaws, founder stock purchase and vesting, 83(b) proof if applicable | Plus board and stockholder consents, prior SAFE/note documents | Plus minutes, shareholder agreements, securities filings |
| Cap table | Clean, fully diluted, ties to signed documents | Plus SAFE/note conversion scenarios | Plus option ledger; investors model dilution themselves |
| IP | Founder IP assignments | Plus employee and contractor assignments, open-source list | Plus trademark/patent filings, third-party licences |
| Contracts | Key customer/vendor terms, NDAs | Material customer and vendor contracts | Full material contracts, DPAs, leases, loans, insurance |
| Financials and KPIs | Bank/burn summary, simple forecast | Monthly statements 12-18 months, runway model, KPI sheet | Two to three years of financials, plan-vs-actual, cohort and unit economics |
| Product, security, privacy | Product overview, hosting and data-flow summary | Privacy policy, subprocessors, security basics | Architecture, security posture/audit evidence, incident history |
| Hiring and ESOP | Team bios | Org chart, offer letters, equity plan draft | Option pool, ledger, employment agreements, contractor agreements |

Sources for stage scoping: [CRV data room setup](https://www.crv.com/content/data-room-setup) (pre-fundraise, active, post-term-sheet lists), [Klehr Harrison](https://klehr.com/publications/startup-evolution-a-year-long-journey-through-a-founders-toolkit-due-diligence-ready-building-a-data-room-that-wins-investor-confidence/) (12-18 months of monthly statements, IP and HR lists), and a Series A checklist vendor summary via search (two to three years of financials; [Haven, undated](https://www.usehaven.com/blog-posts/series-a-data-room-checklist)). The column split is **Inference**; sources disagree on exact items.

### 6.3 Access control and tooling

- Layer access: view-only deck with an expiry, then summary and headline financials, then core corporate and financial documents after NDA or serious interest, then sensitive contracts and cohort data for the lead only ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/data-room-setup); [Klehr Harrison](https://klehr.com/publications/startup-evolution-a-year-long-journey-through-a-founders-toolkit-due-diligence-ready-building-a-data-room-that-wins-investor-confidence/)).
- Use a virtual data room with audit logs and per-investor links, not public file links; watermark sensitive PDFs and disable bulk download on the most sensitive layer (Klehr Harrison).
- Refresh monthly and revoke access after the round closes (Klehr Harrison).
- Tooling options (not endorsed): DocSend, a paid virtual data room, or a permissioned Drive/Dropbox folder. **Inference:** at pre-seed a permissioned folder plus a DocSend-style deck link is usually enough; check what your jurisdiction and the investor require.

### 6.4 Red flags that surface in diligence

- Cap table does not tie to signed SAFEs, notes, grants and approvals; share counts differ between the cap table, model and legal ledger ([Techstars, 2026-08-17](https://www.techstars.com/blog/founder-advice/the-legal-red-flags-that-can-slow-down-your-seed-round); [Klehr Harrison](https://klehr.com/publications/startup-evolution-a-year-long-journey-through-a-founders-toolkit-due-diligence-ready-building-a-data-room-that-wins-investor-confidence/)).
- Founder stock not properly issued, or missing 83(b) elections (30-day deadline; US-specific) (Techstars).
- Missing IP assignments from founders, employees or contractors, including outside dev teams (Techstars; Klehr Harrison).
- Informal advisor or contractor arrangements and verbal equity promises (Techstars).
- Deck figures that do not match accounting files, e.g. ARR; messy versioning ("final FINAL") ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/content/data-room-setup)).
- Public or unrestricted links to sensitive documents (Klehr Harrison).

**So what for a solo founder:** Fix cap table, IP assignment and founder-stock paperwork before the first meeting (as a solo founder, your own assignment and any contractor's are the whole IP chain); everything else can wait for the term sheet. Have a startup lawyer confirm jurisdiction-specific items.

## Checklist

- [ ] One-sentence description tested on three strangers.
- [ ] Core deck of 10-12 slides; appendix holds the rest.
- [ ] Read-alone and presented versions both exist.
- [ ] Traction slide labels actual vs assumed; cohorts shown if retention exists.
- [ ] Ask states amount, runway months and 2-4 milestones.
- [ ] Market built bottom-up with arithmetic visible.
- [ ] Team slide answers the solo-founder question explicitly.
- [ ] 2-3 minute pitch rehearsed against a timer; 20 questions answered with numbers.
- [ ] Cap table ties to signed documents; IP assignments signed by everyone.
- [ ] Data room folders created, version-stamped and access-layered; no public links.
- [ ] Lawyer has reviewed corporate, IP and equity items before the first term sheet.
