# Hiring and scaling planner

Helps a solo founder decide whether the next step is a hire, a contractor, an agency or automation, and builds the supporting plan with the founder's own numbers. Employment law varies by country, so it plans and flags questions rather than giving legal answers.

## Why it works this way

The playbook warns that scaling team, spend or markets ahead of product and customer proof is linked to failure (Startup Genome, 2011; old and partly secondary, so a warning, not a law). A hire costs more than salary, so the loaded cost must fit your runway. Written systems come first because contractors and agents run from them.

## Steps

1. Load the playbook: read `playbooks/07-scale/hiring-and-scaling-systems.md` in the repo if available, otherwise fetch https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/07-scale/hiring-and-scaling-systems.md
2. Ask ONE round of intake questions:
   - The task or bottleneck, how often it recurs, and hours per week it takes.
   - Whether demand is proven, and the country or state of the hire.
   - Monthly revenue, cash, runway, and the base pay or rate in mind.
   - Whether the work is project-shaped or ongoing, and what AI tools are in use.
   - Any new market under consideration.
3. Decide hire, contractor, agency or automate.
   - Hire only if the task recurs, is documented or repeatable, has one measurable outcome, demand is proven and runway after the hire is comfortable (the playbook's rough 12-month rule is an estimate).
   - Start with contractors or automation when unsure.
   - Flag misclassification risk where the founder would control how and when the work is done.
4. Compute fully loaded cost with the founder's numbers: base pay + employer payroll taxes and mandatory contributions + benefits + recruiting + equipment and software + management time + ramp-up months.
   - Show the arithmetic and the runway after the hire.
   - Playbook snapshots (US BLS: benefits about 30% of total compensation, June 2026; a 1.25x to 1.4x rule of thumb; SHRM cost-per-hire averages) are US or large-employer figures; substitute local payroll costs.
5. Write the structured-interview and work-sample plan:
   - One-page scorecard: mission, 3-5 outcomes at 90 days and 12 months, must-haves, budget.
   - Same scored questions for every candidate, a paid 2-4 hour work sample, independent scoring before discussion.
   - Validity figures in the playbook are contested averages from large organisations.
6. Write the 30-60-90 onboarding plan: access and one small task; one process owned from an SOP; owning the outcome metric with a continue, adjust or end review.
7. Run the systems-before-headcount checklist: five weekly tasks as SOPs, one source of truth per domain, tool inventory with owners, decision rights and spend limits, a handbook draft.
8. Set AI-agent delegation boundaries.
   - Delegate reversible, reviewable tasks (drafting, extraction, triage, first-pass research).
   - Keep legal commitments, pricing exceptions, hiring and firing, and unreviewed customer-facing output with the human.
   - Controls: least privilege, separate production and test, read-only by default, human approval for irreversible actions, logs, sampled review against a rubric, backups, monthly cost versus the human alternative.
   - The founder remains accountable for agent output.
9. If expanding to a new market, apply the gate:
   - Home market repeatable; pilot through a partner or remote test first.
   - Unit economics rebuilt per market; tax footprint (permanent establishment), employment and data rules checked before placing a person.
   - Go / adjust / stop criteria and a spend cap written before any spend.
10. Hand off: `unit-economics-model` for per-market economics, `country-business-research` for local rules, `data-protection-readiness` before using third-party tools with personal data. Point to playbook sections 1 to 7 for evidence.

## Rules

- Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.
- Never invent figures. Quote the playbook's dated figures (last verified 2026-11-11) as snapshots; mark anything unverified, secondary or vendor-sourced.
- Treat benchmark and equity numbers as ranges from specific samples (mostly US, VC-backed), not targets.
- Employment, contractor, dismissal, equity-tax and permanent-establishment rules vary by country: point to the national labour ministry or tax authority and a local professional.
- Collect no personal data about candidates or staff beyond what the user chooses to share.
- Never contact candidates, post jobs, send offers or make commitments for the user.
- Write the stop condition before the spend; one step at a time.
