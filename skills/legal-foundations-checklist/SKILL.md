# Legal foundations checklist

This skill turns the contracts, IP and equity playbook into a status checklist the founder can work through with a lawyer. It never drafts contract text as final and never claims a template is valid in the user's jurisdiction. It is research and planning support only, not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.

## Why it works this way

The cheapest legal steps on day one (shares in writing, founder IP assignment, contractor assignments) are the most expensive to fix later, and diligence by investors tends to find them. A status table makes gaps visible, and a lawyer is then used for the high-risk items instead of boilerplate.

## Steps

1. **Load the playbook.** Read `playbooks/02-foundation/contracts-ip-and-equity.md` in the repo if available; otherwise fetch https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/02-foundation/contracts-ip-and-equity.md. Note its "Last verified" date.
2. **Ask one round of intake questions** (all at once, let the user skip any):
   - Country of incorporation and where the founder lives.
   - Stage: not incorporated, incorporated, or already have customers or investors.
   - Solo or co-founders; are shares issued yet and is any vesting in place?
   - Who has written code, design or content so far (founder before incorporation, contractors, AI tools)?
   - Name, domain and trademark status; markets where you sell or raise.
   - Do you handle personal data; do you plan a first hire or contractor?
3. **Equity and vesting.** From playbook section 1, explain the common convention (four-year vesting, one-year cliff) as a convention to discuss, and why investors may still ask a sole founder to vest. If the company is US-based and shares vest, flag the 83(b) 30-day deadline and tell the user to confirm with a US tax adviser. Treat advisor and hire equity figures as sanity checks from vendor data, not entitlements. This is not legal advice; confirm with a qualified lawyer.
4. **IP.** From playbook section 2, check:
   - The founder IP assignment (including domains and accounts) and any prior-employer claims.
   - Contractor and employee assignments signed before work starts.
   - Open-source licence inventory and trade-secret hygiene.
   - Human authorship records for AI-assisted work; rules differ by country and are fast-moving, so mark positions the playbook leaves unverified.
5. **Names and trademarks.** From playbook section 3: knock-out search, domain and handle checks, then the filing office for their main market. Quote office fees with their dates as snapshots and tell the user to confirm on the official office page (UKIPO, EUIPO, USPTO, WIPO). Clearance judgement belongs to a trademark attorney.
6. **Contracts in priority order.** Use playbook section 4.2: customer terms, order form, NDAs, contractor agreement, SOW, DPA and privacy policy, website terms, acceptable-use policy, refund policy.
   - For each, say what it is for and the clauses to understand (liability cap, indemnity, IP, termination, payment, governing law).
   - Name open template sources with their licence limits only as starting points.
   - Do not draft final text and do not say any template is valid in the user's jurisdiction.
7. **Worker classification.** From playbook section 5, list red flags.
   - Contractor signals: sets own hours and methods, own tools, several clients, can send a substitute, paid per deliverable.
   - Employee signals: close control, integration into the business, fixed hours.
   - Point to the national authority's official test. The playbook covers US (IRS, DOL) and UK (HMRC) only; for other countries say so and ask the user to find the local guidance.
8. **Deliver.**
   - A status table with every item marked done, not started or unclear.
   - A next-three-actions list.
   - The 15 questions to bring to a startup lawyer from playbook section 6, adjusted to the answers.
9. **Hand off.** Say what only a lawyer can settle (equity, IP assignment, financing, high-liability contracts) and which skill comes next (`company-setup-navigator`, `data-protection-readiness`).

## Rules

- Research and planning support only. This is NOT legal, tax, investment or financial advice; repeat this beside the equity, IP and contract steps.
- Tell the user to confirm with the named official body and a qualified local lawyer.
- Never invent fees, thresholds, statutes, case names or registry names. Quote the playbook's figures with their date and say they are snapshots that must be re-checked.
- Mark anything unverified, including playbook items marked snippet-only or open question.
- Never draft contract text as final, and never claim a template is valid in the user's jurisdiction.
- Never sign, file or submit anything for the user.
- Ask for no personal data beyond what the user chooses to share.
