# SEO and GEO launch plan

Turns the SEO and GEO playbook into a plan for one specific site. The output is a checklist, a small content plan, a measurement sheet and a 90-day schedule, with each action marked as evidenced or speculative.

## Why it works this way

Google's own documentation says AI Overviews and AI Mode need no special markup beyond sound SEO, so the basics are also the GEO baseline. Most other GEO advice is correlation data from tool vendors. A new site wins by going narrow and first-hand, and by being mentioned across the web, not by volume.

## Steps

1. **Load the playbook.**
   - Read `playbooks/04-launch/seo-and-geo-playbook.md` in the repo if available; otherwise fetch https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/04-launch/seo-and-geo-playbook.md .
   - Note its "Last verified" date.
2. **Ask one round of intake questions.**
   - Ask all at once; short answers are fine: site URL and stack or CMS; what the product solves and for whom; target country and language; local or online business; 3 to 5 known competitors; analytics and Search Console status; hours per week available.
   - Do not continue until answered.
3. **Technical pass (playbook Section 2, checklist).**
   - Go through: Search Console and Bing verified, sitemap, HTTPS and mobile, no accidental noindex or robots blocks, internal links, Core Web Vitals thresholds, canonicals, Organization/Product/Article markup matching visible text.
   - Do not recommend FAQ schema for Google (deprecated per the playbook).
   - Mark each item pass / fail / unknown.
4. **Topic cluster (Section 3).**
   - Propose one narrow problem and 10 to 20 interlinked pages, each mapped to one search intent.
   - Favour specific, comparison, local and first-hand angles over informational head terms.
   - Name one original-data asset the founder could produce.
   - Flag scaled or unreviewed AI pages as a spam risk.
5. **GEO/AEO plan (Section 4).**
   - Split into three lists labelled Fact (platform documentation, for example do not block search bots, llms.txt is not used by Google Search), Inference (clear entity facts, quotable answers, genuine third-party mentions) and Vendor hype to discount.
   - Add the off-site actions from Section 5 that need no paid links or fake mentions.
6. **Measurement (Sections 4 and 6).**
   - Provide a baseline sheet, a 15 to 25 prompt test list (run each three times, monthly) across the engines the founder cares about, and the referral and log checks.
7. **90-day plan (Sections 6 and 8).**
   - Days 1-30, 31-60, 61-90, sized to the founder's weekly hours.
   - State realistic expectations from the playbook and a second acquisition channel.
8. **Deliver and hand off.**
   - Give the checklist, cluster map, GEO table, tracking sheet and schedule.
   - Cite playbook section numbers.
   - Suggest `go-to-market-planner` for channel choice and `network-builder` for off-site mentions.

## Rules

- Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.
- Never invent figures. Quote the playbook's dated figures with their date, say they are snapshots, and say this area moves fast, so re-check before relying on them.
- Treat benchmark numbers (click-rate drops, ranking odds, citation overlap) as ranges, not targets.
- Mark anything unverified, including vendor claims and snippet-only sources.
- Use public professional information only.
- Never post, submit, email or contact anyone for the user; respect search-engine spam policies and platform rules. Do not suggest paid links, fake reviews or manufactured mentions.
- Do not promise rankings, citations or traffic.
- Re-check Google documentation before acting on any item the playbook lists under Open questions.
