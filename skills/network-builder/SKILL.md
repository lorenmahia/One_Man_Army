# Network builder

Turns the networking playbook into a 90-day plan for a founder with no network: who matters, where they gather, how to approach them respectfully, and how to record it lightly.

## Why it works this way

Moderately distant ties and warm introductions tend to help most, but published multipliers conflict by 10x or more and mostly come from vendors, so the plan relies on direction, not numbers. Advisor equity and partnerships are small, time-boxed and standardised, which limits risk for a solo founder.

## Steps

1. **Load the playbook.**
   - Read `playbooks/04-launch/networking-communities-and-partnerships.md` in the repo if available; otherwise fetch https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/04-launch/networking-communities-and-partnerships.md .
   - Note its "Last verified" date.
2. **Ask one round of intake questions.**
   - Ask: the venture and stage; country and language; the single most urgent need (customers, advisors, talent, investors, partners, press); who they already know, even loosely; hours per week; anything they will not do (for example events or public posting).
3. **Stakeholder map (Section 2).**
   - For each need, list 10 to 30 role types, not people.
   - Rate influence, access difficulty and what the founder can offer.
   - Choose 2 to 3 role types per need for 90 days and name the bridge organisations that contain many of them.
4. **Target list.**
   - Using public professional information only (company pages, registries, conference speaker lists, published work), propose named organisations first and individuals only where the public role is clear.
   - Suggest search routes; do not scrape, buy lists or infer private attributes.
   - For a new country, use `country-business-research`.
5. **Community shortlist.**
   - Pick 3 to 5 communities, one per major need.
   - Score each against the playbook's good signals and red flags, and ask the founder to verify by talking to two members the organiser did not select.
   - Compare cost in money, equity, time and exclusivity; equity terms need a lawyer.
6. **Outreach cadence (Section 3).**
   - Plan double opt-in intros first, then individual messages, with one follow-up after 3 to 7 days and at most one more, then stop.
   - Add a give-first ledger.
   - Hand drafting to `founder-outreach-drafts`.
   - State the lawful-outreach checkpoints (US, UK, EU, other) as items to confirm with official sources.
7. **Advisors (Section 4).**
   - Show the equity norms as dated ranges with their sources and the vendor interest of each, plus vesting and cliff.
   - Propose two or three advisors with named gaps, a one-page scope and a time-boxed trial.
8. **Partnership pilot (Section 5).**
   - Define a goal with a number, a 30 to 90 day time-box, scope, success criteria, non-exclusive terms and a clean exit.
9. **Tracker and schedule (Section 7).**
   - Give the contact-tracker fields (organisation, role type, source, last contact, legal-basis note, opt-out flag, stage, next action, gave, asked, related need) and a Week 1 / Month 1 / Quarter 1 plan, labelled as planning estimates.

## Rules

- Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice. Advisor and partner agreements need a lawyer.
- Never invent figures. Quote the playbook's dated figures with their date, say they are snapshots, and say rules and norms change, so re-check before relying on them.
- Treat reply rates, intro multipliers and equity numbers as ranges.
- Mark anything unverified, including snippet-only sources.
- Public professional information only; no private data, no sensitive-attribute inference, no personal examples in the tracker.
- Never send messages, post, join groups or contact anyone for the user. Respect anti-spam law, opt-outs and platform rules, including no automation where a platform bans it.
- Be honest about stage; never imply partnerships or endorsements that do not exist.
