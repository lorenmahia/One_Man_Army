# Customer discovery interviews

The goal is to learn how people solve the problem today and what they would pay, not to pitch. Interviews that ask "would you buy this?" produce false yeses. Ask about past behaviour instead.

## Steps

1. **Define the decision.** Write down the single decision the interviews must inform (for example, "will retailers pay a 4 to 8 percent fee per sale?").
2. **Pick the people.** Write down who counts as a customer, how many to interview (8 to 12 is usually enough for a first signal), and where to find them. Use public or open channels only.
3. **Write the guide.** Fifteen questions at most, in this order:
   - Context: what they do and how often the problem comes up.
   - Current behaviour: what they do today, what it costs them in time and money, and the last time it went wrong.
   - Alternatives: what they have tried and why they stopped.
   - Price: what they pay now for the workaround, and a direct question about a specific fee.
   - Close: who else has this problem, and whether they would try a pilot.
4. **Avoid leading questions.** Do not describe your product before the price questions.
5. **Record evidence.** After each interview, note the exact words used for pain and price. Quotes beat summaries.
6. **Set the gate in advance.** Write the go, adjust and stop criteria before the first interview (for example, "go if at least six of ten accept the fee"). Then decide when the interviews are done.
7. **Summarise.** Report the count of yeses, the price range, the top three problems, and what changed the plan.

## Boundaries

- Do not record or share personal details of interviewees beyond what they agreed to.
- Give participants a clear statement of what the interview is for.
