# Pitch deck builder

The purpose is to turn what the founder already knows into a deck outline where every slide has evidence behind it, plus rehearsed answers and a data-room checklist. It can then build the deck with whatever tooling the user has.

## Why it works this way

A deck exists to win the next meeting, and investors skim it in a few minutes, so team and traction must be visible fast.
A solo founder should answer the single-founder concern before it is asked.
Cap table and IP paperwork are the usual causes of diligence delay, so they are fixed before the first meeting.

## Steps

1. **Load the playbook.** Read `playbooks/05-raise/pitch-deck-and-data-room.md` in the repo if available; otherwise fetch `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/05-raise/pitch-deck-and-data-room.md`. Note its "Last verified" date.
2. **Ask one round of intake questions:**
   - One-sentence description of the company, and the stage.
   - The named customer and the problem evidence (interviews).
   - Product state, and any demo or screenshots.
   - Traction: paying customers who are not friends, revenue trend, retention, LOIs or pilots.
   - Price and rough unit economics; market size as customers x price x frequency.
   - Why solo, and who fills the gaps.
   - The raise: amount, runway months, milestones; jurisdiction.
   - Whether the deck will be presented, read alone, or both.
3. **Propose the slide order** from playbook section 2.3 (12 slides: title, problem, solution, why now, traction, business model, bottom-up market, competition, go-to-market, solo team, ask and milestones, optional vision). Say it is a synthesis, not any firm's template.
4. **Fill an evidence checklist per slide:** what investors expect, what the founder has, what is missing, and the common mistake to avoid.
   - Label every metric as actual, estimated or assumed.
   - Flag cumulative signups, "1% of a huge market" claims and a missing solo-founder answer.
5. **Check the ask** (section 4): amount, months of runway, two to four measurable milestones, and the Series A evidence they produce. Leave valuation and terms off the deck by default. Offer `unit-economics-model` for the numbers.
6. **Prepare Q&A.** Use the 20 questions in section 5.2.
   - Draft answer skeletons from the founder's own facts.
   - Mark gaps where an answer needs a number or a document.
   - Prioritise questions 4, 5, 7, 11 and 19.
7. **Data room.** Give the folder structure (section 6.1), the checklist column for the user's stage (section 6.2), the layered-access advice (6.3) and the red flags (6.4).
   - Fix cap table, IP assignments and founder stock before the first meeting.
   - Have a startup lawyer confirm jurisdiction-specific items.
8. **Offer to build the deck.** Ask what tooling they have (slides software, a deck skill or plugin, or plain markdown) and produce an outline or draft in that format. Keep presented slides short and put depth in an appendix. Treat third-party tools as optional and check their output against the playbook.
9. **Hand off:** `customer-discovery-interviews` for evidence gaps, `investor-research-pipeline` for targets, `founder-outreach-drafts` for outreach.

## Rules

- Research and planning support only; this is not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.
- Never invent traction, customers, quotes or numbers. Quote the playbook's dated figures (such as DocSend reading times) with their date, say they are snapshots, and mark anything unverified.
- Treat benchmarks (deck length, reading time, seed ARR) as ranges; the playbook notes they conflict across sources.
- Use only information the founder provides or public professional sources. Never send, upload, share or sign anything on the user's behalf.
- Open the data room late and in layers; never suggest public file links for sensitive documents.
