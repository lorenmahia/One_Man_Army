# Operating rhythm setup

Builds the small set of management routines that transfer to a team of one, and leaves out the machinery meant for coordinating people. The founder ends with a written set they can start this week.

## Why it works this way

The playbook found no independent, rigorous evidence that OKR, EOS or Scaling Up systems cause better results; the claims come from books, vendors and consultancies. So this skill borrows only cheap primitives: a few written priorities, weekly input metrics, a monthly written report, written decisions and pre-agreed kill criteria. Solo founders face key-person risk and sunk-cost drift, which these address.

## Steps

1. Load the playbook: read `playbooks/06-operate/operating-cadences-okrs-and-reporting.md` in the repo if available, otherwise fetch https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/06-operate/operating-cadences-okrs-and-reporting.md
2. Ask ONE round of intake questions:
   - Stage (pre-revenue, early revenue, scaling) and the revenue model.
   - Current numbers and where each lives (payments, bank, analytics).
   - The one to three outcomes that matter this quarter.
   - Whether investors or advisors get updates, and weekly hours available.
   - Cash and runway today, and any single point of failure (one customer, one vendor, only you).
3. Be honest up front: say the evidence for named systems is weak, then offer the primitives below as a menu.
4. Draft quarterly OKRs:
   - One to three objectives, two to four numeric key results each.
   - A baseline for each, or "establish the baseline" as the first KR.
   - Committed versus stretch labelled, and a scoring rule agreed before the quarter.
   - Playbook examples are illustrative, not benchmarks; label any example you show.
5. Pick the weekly scorecard:
   - 5 to 10 metrics, input separated from output, one written definition and one source of truth each.
   - Weekly review template in a 30 to 45 minute box: customer, scorecard exceptions, why, OKR check, cash, risks and decisions, next week's top 3.
6. Draft the monthly close and update outline:
   - Headline (green / yellow / red), metrics against last month and plan, up to three specific asks, highlights and lowlights with causes, next month's priorities.
   - Bad-month rule: lead with the miss, state cause and runway, give the plan, make one ask.
   - Worth sending even with no investors.
7. Create the decision log and risk register:
   - Decision log: date, decision, one-way or two-way, options considered, why, expected outcome, review date.
   - Five-row risk register: likelihood, impact, early warning, mitigation, review date; include key-person risk and a one-page continuity note.
   - Offer a pre-mortem on the quarter's biggest bet.
8. Write go / adjust / stop kill criteria and three runway scenarios. Time-box and runway thresholds are illustrative inferences; the founder sets and dates their own.
9. Deliver the founder's set as one document, then offer (do not assume):
   - A dashboard via `artifact-project-dashboard`.
   - Scheduled refresh via `scheduled-artifact-refresh`.
   - Review and deadline calendar items via `deadline-calendar-sync`.
   - Automate collecting and reminding; the founder keeps the deciding. Cite playbook sections 2 to 6.

## Rules

- Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.
- Never invent figures. Quote the playbook's dated figures (last verified 2026-11-11) as snapshots; mark anything unverified, secondary or vendor-sourced.
- Treat benchmark numbers (CAC payback, gross margin, retention) as ranges from larger companies, not targets.
- Always label example OKRs, metrics and thresholds as illustrative.
- Do not claim OKR, EOS or Scaling Up improve results.
- Verify every number against its source system; do not invent explanations for anomalies.
- Collect no personal data beyond what the user shares.
- Never send updates, contact investors or advisors, create calendar items or publish anything without the user's explicit go-ahead.
- Advisor equity and legal terms need a lawyer's review.
