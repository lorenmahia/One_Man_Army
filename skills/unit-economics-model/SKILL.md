# Unit economics model

The purpose is to show where a product makes or loses money and which assumption matters most. Every number is an assumption until it comes from real data, so label them.

## Steps

1. **List the assumptions.** Price or fee per unit, units per period, cost of goods or service per unit, losses or defaults or refunds, payment and platform fees, fixed monthly costs, and any capital tied up (float, inventory). Mark each as "assumed", "sourced" or "from interviews".
2. **Per-unit view.** Revenue minus variable costs equals contribution per unit. Show it as an amount and a percentage.
3. **Scale view.** Multiply by volume and subtract fixed costs to get monthly and annual profit. State the break-even volume.
4. **Capital view.** Work out how much capital is tied up at peak and how fast it is recycled. This often decides feasibility more than margin.
5. **Sensitivity.** Run three cases (base, pessimistic, optimistic) and show which single assumption moves the result most. Show a small table, not a long narrative.
6. **Verdict.** State whether the model works at the assumed numbers, what it would take to work, and which assumption to test first.

## Rules

- Keep the currency explicit, and show the exchange rate and its date if the currency is not the user's.
- Do not present assumed figures as facts.
- Name the two or three assumptions that would change the decision.
- Keep the model simple enough for the user to rebuild it in a spreadsheet.
