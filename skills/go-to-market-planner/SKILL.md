# Go-to-market planner

Turns the go-to-market playbook into a one-venture plan: who to sell to first, how to say it, what to charge, where to find them, how to close ten customers by hand, and when to stop or scale.

## Why it works this way

Early traction comes from one narrow segment and one channel, sold by the founder personally, not from broad campaigns. Benchmarks in this area are noisy and vendor-biased, so the plan uses them as sanity ranges and sets the decision gate before launch so a bad month cannot be rationalised.

## Steps

1. **Load the playbook.**
   - Read `playbooks/04-launch/go-to-market-and-first-customers.md` in the repo if available; otherwise fetch https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lorenmahia/One_Man_Army/main/playbooks/04-launch/go-to-market-and-first-customers.md .
   - Note its "Last verified" date.
2. **Ask one round of intake questions.**
   - Ask: what the product is and its stage; business type (B2B SaaS, B2C app, marketplace, local or service, hardware, emerging-market mobile-first); candidate customer groups; what buyers use today (including spreadsheets or nothing); any customer conversations so far; country and payment or messaging rails; runway and hours per week.
3. **ICP and beachhead (Section 1).**
   - Write a one-sentence ICP with 3 to 5 observable filters.
   - Score two or three candidate segments on pain, reachability, serviceability, references and willingness to pay.
   - Check the 100-named-prospects test and a bottom-up size with labelled assumptions.
   - Pick a provisional beachhead.
4. **Positioning canvas (Section 2).**
   - Fill the six boxes in order, naming the alternative being replaced.
   - Propose the five-second, interview, commitment and switching-cost tests.
   - Mark proof points as missing until a real customer supplies them.
5. **Pricing approach (Section 3).**
   - Compare value-based, cost-plus and competitor anchors.
   - Recommend a paid pilot (30 to 90 days, written success metric) over a free tier unless the founder has a reason.
   - Plan to quote a real price in every call and vary it.
   - Hand the cost floor and CAC payback to `unit-economics-model`.
6. **Channel shortlist (Section 4).**
   - For the stated business type pick one primary channel and one backup, with the failure modes and the playbook's metric ranges shown as ranges.
   - Define a 20-experiment test for the primary.
7. **First-10-customers plan (Section 5).**
   - Prospect list, daily outreach volume, discovery call outline, pilot terms, objection log, referral ask.
   - Point to `customer-discovery-interviews` and `founder-outreach-drafts`.
8. **Launch checklist and gate (Sections 6 and 7).**
   - Give the checklist, then write the go / adjust / stop table with the founder's own thresholds, agreed now.
   - Label the playbook's example thresholds as illustrative placeholders, not benchmarks.
9. **Deliver.**
   - One document: ICP, canvas, price plan, channel, 30/60/90 actions, checklist, gate.
   - Cite playbook sections.

## Rules

- Research and planning support only; not legal, tax, investment or financial advice.
- Never invent figures. Quote the playbook's dated figures with their date, say they are snapshots, and say conversion, CAC and reply-rate numbers move fast, so re-check before relying on them.
- Treat benchmark numbers as ranges, never targets; note when sources conflict.
- Mark anything unverified, including secondary or snippet-only sources.
- Use public professional information only when building prospect lists.
- Never send messages, post launches or contact anyone for the user; respect anti-spam rules and each platform's self-promotion rules.
- Local payment, messaging-consent and licensing rules need local legal advice.
- Do not move to scale unless the gate says go on at least three rows.
